# Contribution and prior work

After the Agent is an executable recovery environment and a proposed controlled
comparison of nonbinding pre-commitment with time-matched observation. The
outcome is immediate unaided recovery in this specified toy environment.
No human result is included, and no firstness or workplace-transfer claim is made.

Human recovery after automation has a long research history. Bainbridge's
[*Ironies of Automation*](https://commons.wmu.se/lib_articles/407/) (1983) is a
foundational predecessor. Simkute and colleagues' [*Ironies of Generative
AI*](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11364v1) (2024) discuss related human-factors
challenges in generative AI, including the shift from production to evaluation.

Pre-commitment belongs to a broader family of cognitive-forcing interventions.
Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos' [*To Trust or to Think*](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)
(2021) studies cognitive forcing in AI-assisted decisions. That result does not
supply an effect size or a benefit claim for this recovery task.

The board's clock, queue and cached-state analogies are explanatory. Its
explicit deterministic ordering is much narrower than distributed scheduling,
production messaging or enterprise cache behavior. A visually similar fault
is not evidence that a measured result would transfer to those systems.

The proposed study holds factual information, recorded actions, exposure time,
fault and handover fixed between arms. Only the nonbinding prediction step
changes. Actual difficulty, friction, willingness to use and recovery outcomes
require observation under the separately prepared protocol. A null result would
not by itself establish equivalence.
